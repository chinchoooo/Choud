Your own cloud. Your own server. Nobody else's.

Choud is a self-hosted Google Drive alternative that runs on one spare Linux machine at home. Your files sit on your own disk, are stored once however often you upload them, and reach your phone and laptops over Tailscale or your home network. No subscription. No tracking.

The landlord problem

Every big cloud drive works the same way: your memories sit in someone else's building, and you pay rent to keep them there. That is not a secret. It is just a deal worth reading twice.

The questionA rented cloud driveChoud
How much room do I get?15 GB free, shared with your mail and photos. Then a monthly bill, for as long as you want your files.Your whole disk. Buy a bigger one once.
Whose computer holds my files?The company's servers.The machine in your house.
Who can lock me out?The company can suspend or close an account under its terms, and the files go with it.Nobody but you.
What if they drop the product?Google has shut down a long list of products. It is their call, on their date.It is your server and your code. MIT licensed, so it keeps running.

YOURS

The middle column comes from Google's published terms and storage plans: check them yourself at policies.google.com/terms. Drive is a good product, and on speed Choud only matches it. The difference is who owns the building.

Pull the plug

Most storage trouble happens when the power dies halfway through saving. Press the button, then pull the plug while the file is being written. Both disks lose power at the same instant.

What you get

Files
Folders, drag-and-drop, resumable uploads (a dropped connection only resends what is missing), a photo timeline, favorites, recents and an activity log.
Never lose anything
Every overwrite is a new version you can restore. Deleted files wait in a trash. Nothing is destroyed on a doubt.
Find anything
Search by file name and by what is written inside documents: text, code, PDF, Word and OpenDocument.
View anywhere
Built-in viewers for images, video, audio, PDF and text, with thumbnails. No other app needed.
Share
Public links with an expiry and a password, view-only or "add and edit". The people you share with need no account, and one command makes links work from anywhere without exposing the rest of your drive. A pause switch turns them all off.
Phone
An Android app backs up photos and videos in the background with the phone's real dates, and resumes if the connection drops. Its Photos tab keeps the whole library on the phone for slow links.
Laptops
A desktop client mirrors a folder both ways, with conflict copies and never a silent overwrite, or quietly backs up folders you choose.
Time travel
Rewind any folder to an earlier moment, preview it, and put it back. An undo for ransomware and bad syncs.
Vault
An encrypted vault sealed in your browser. The server never sees the contents, or even the file names.
People
Invite family and friends with one-time codes. Each person gets their own storage and quota.
Safe by default
Optional two-factor login, a registration lock, rate limiting, hash-checked uploads, nightly verified backups and webhook alerts.

How it is built

Two halves, each doing what it is best at. The Python API owns accounts, permissions, versions, sharing and search. The C++ engine owns hashing, storage by content, deduplication and assembling chunks. The engine is never reachable from the network: only the API talks to it.

  • Crash-tested storage. A file is flushed to disk before it gets its final name. The tests kill the process mid-write on purpose and check that no half-written file ever appears.
  • Checked, not trusted. Hashes are verified, paths are confined to the storage folder, and the request parser is fuzzed and run under sanitizers.
  • Public links stay narrow. Share links are served by a second door that can only reach shared files, never your account or the rest of the API.

Run your own

You need one Linux machine with Docker. An old laptop is plenty.

git clone https://github.com/chinchoooo/Choud
cd Choud
scripts/setup.sh      # creates .env with strong random secrets
scripts/deploy.sh     # checks the machine, builds, starts, waits until healthy

Then install Tailscale on the server and your devices, and open the server's address. The full guide, including backups and public links, is in docs/deployment.md.

What it is not, yet

  • The Android app has had little time on a real phone. There is no iOS app.
  • You can share by link only. Sharing with named accounts is not built.
  • It is built for one household on one machine, not for thousands of users.
  • It has not had an independent security audit.

The plans and the history are in docs/roadmap.md and the changelog.
